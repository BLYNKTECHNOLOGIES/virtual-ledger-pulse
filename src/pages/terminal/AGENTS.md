# Copilot decisions (v2 Playbook)

- Copilot AI runs only for Copilot approvers (copilot_config.approver_ids, checked by copilot_is_approver) and only for the order chat an approver has open: the chat panel calls copilot-draft, nothing runs in the background, because AI spend and Playbook training are limited to chats the approvers handle. The one exception is rewording (copilot-rephrase), which also serves the operators an approver picks, and only while they type in an open chat.
- copilot_my_access feeds the screen; every Copilot gate in the UI reads it and copilot-draft / copilot-learn check again, because screen and server must agree.
- Approved Playbook replies (copilot_match_playbook, scoped by situation, side, small/regular, account and client state; older replies by status) are shown before any AI and skip drafting; the most specific fitting reply per question wins, then the one used in the most similar chat; one chat state (last message, status, situation) is drafted once (copilot_requests.request_key), so two approvers never pay twice for the same message.
- Sending or approving a suggestion saves it to the Playbook through copilot-learn ("use = approve"; Undo within 24 h, only while it is the entry's latest change); own typed replies only when Save is on; rejected drafts are kept as examples to avoid, never as replies.
- No reply text carries a typed number: numbers come from copilot_contacts through {contact:<team>} (teams are rows of copilot_teams; available-now first), order values through {amount} {orderNo} {name} {upi}; guardDraft holds back release promises (English and Hinglish), outside-Binance offers, typed numbers and, on our sell orders before verification, anything that invites payment; the composer refuses to send an unfilled placeholder, because a client must never see a wrong number or a placeholder.
- A number is given only when the client asks for one: the KYC team's to a new client still in verification (the one-time verification call is the KYC team's, never the relationship team's), the Relationship team's once KYC is done.
- Personal data in the chat (phones, UPI IDs, account numbers, PAN, Aadhaar, references, wallet addresses, transaction hashes) is masked with maskPII before any AI call and in stored questions (text read through normalizeText first: Devanagari and full-width digits, invisible characters; masked before any cut); the screen's maskPersonal (a question an approver adds) must give the same result (maskParity.test.ts, samples and a fuzz), because the Playbook must never hold what the AI would not be shown. maskPersonal is written without lookbehind (older Safari cannot parse it): a check on what comes before a match lives in code, and a refused match lets the search go on one character later. The digit rules are such checks (longRun, groupedRun in copilot-playbook.ts, copied word for word into copilotText.ts): nine digits in a row, or ten or more in groups whatever the separators (a phone typed digit by digit), or nine in groups sharing one separator with a group of three; a decimal number and a date (with its hour) are not values, and a comma never joins digits into an amount. UPI handles for one-letter or spaced IDs are matched by family (Google Pay "ok…", Paytm "pt…", bank names) and the same list lives on both sides. Logs hold an offered rewrite built from its tokens (each value as its kind), never the text masked again, and generalize never puts {name} inside a UPI ID or an email.
- Language and address are decided on the server from the chat: Hindi in English letters only after the client keeps writing Hindi past hindi_switch_after English replies; "ma'am" only when the client refers to herself as female, never from a name.
- The Copilot never skips or suppresses KYC; it shows the client's KYC status and the rule comes from the Playbook.
- Operators get no suggestions and never change the Playbook; copilot_config.operators_enabled stays false until an approver switches on approved replies (a separate step). The operators an approver picks (copilot_config.rephrase_user_ids) can have their own words reworded; their sent rewrites reach the Playbook only when an approver saves them from the inbox.
- Rephrase (copilot-rephrase): rewords what is typed in an open order chat's message box for approvers and the operators an approver picked (copilot_can_rephrase: listed and able to read order chats), only for orders on the person's own Binance accounts (binance_accounts_for_user). It runs after a 1.8 s pause (each later one in a message waits longer and needs three new words; at most 4 per message; never for short text, a finished "Sir, … ." reply or text the screen placed) or from the ✨ button; while the chat stays open, the same words in the same language are answered from the screen's cache, not asked again. Personal values become ⟦n⟧ tokens before the model (maskPII's patterns, repeated until nothing changes) and come back after; the typed words, chat and earlier rewrites reach the model as material, never as instructions. The checks (_shared/copilot-rephrase.ts with copilot-numbers.ts; the hard cases in copilot-rephrase.cases.test.ts) fail closed, because a wrong rewrite costs more than the operator's own words: held back when it uses another script's letters; changes, adds, drops or swaps a value, a number (with its unit and currency) or a placeholder, or adds a time frame; types a value, link or handle; mentions WhatsApp, Telegram or dealing outside Binance, whatever the typed words said; states a payment received (or not), sent, the KYC done or the crypto released that the typed words do not state (a question or a condition states nothing); on our sell orders brings up releasing unless typed, or promises it unless the operator promised it; brings up a call, skipping KYC, someone else's account, cancelling, a refund, an appeal, the police or a block; drops a condition; before verification on our sell orders (or with the side or step unknown) brings in a way of paying the typed words did not; or runs much longer. Where the operator's own words asked to pay or promised the release, it is offered with a warning. Every rewrite that passes these checks is then read by a second call against the typed words, both with values as tokens (buildVerifyMessages/readVerify/applyVerify; copilot_config.rephrase_verify, default on; utility_model; logged as purpose 'judge', variant 'rephrase_check'): anything it finds, or no usable answer, and nothing is offered. copilot-rephrase.review.test.ts holds the second safety review's cases; KNOWN lists the ones only the second call can catch (each must still be a miss for the rules, so the list stays true). The screen keeps rewrites per words (case counts) and language only while the chat stands still: a new message, the order's status or KYC, or the Copilot panel's request (an approver changing the step) makes them be asked again, and an answer that arrives after such a change is dropped. Nothing is sent: Use (Tab) fills the box; Esc hides the strip, then brings back the typed words ("Bring back my words" on touch screens), then leaves the box, one press at a time; Ctrl/Cmd+Enter never approves the rough words while their rewrite is on show; Tab, Esc and Ctrl+Enter belong to an input method while it composes a word (src/lib/copilot/composerKeys.ts). The model (rephrase_model) is asked for no thinking (reasoning_effort none, then low), each call logged as purpose 'rephrase' with the typed words masked; a daily allowance per person (rephrase_daily_limit) and the monthly cap apply. A rewrite sent from the box counts only while what is sent is still mostly the rewrite (a Copilot suggestion reworded stays that suggestion; a rewrite of a rewrite keeps the first typed words): an approver's is learned like a sent suggestion with the typed words as its note (kept on the example chat for decision options later; the saving judge never reads it), an operator's goes to the inbox (kind operator_reply, one open item per order and reply, newest first; the client's question is what came before our sent message in the order's chat; dismissed one at a time or all older than 7 days through copilot_dismiss_operator_replies, approvers only, by the server's clock) and is learned the same way when an approver saves it. Only an offered rewrite (status ok, kept output) can be reported as used, and the server refuses mode 'auto' while rephrase_auto is off.
- Tentative entries (the one-time seed, source 'seed') are offered to approvers only and stop being offered after tentative_days unless used; they must never reach operators, because nobody has approved them.
- The Playbook page (/terminal/playbook) and its sidebar item exist only for approvers (copilot_my_access); inbox decisions go through copilot_inbox_decide and copilot_resolve_conflict, so an entry and its inbox item always change together.
- The situation (what the client is waiting for: copilot_situations codes such as sell.kyc.pending, sell.kyc.call, sell.pay.ready, buy.pay.cooling) is worked out on the server from Binance status and events and our scripted messages (_shared/copilot-situation.ts). An approver's change (copilot_set_order_situation) holds until our next step; client messages never undo it, and the Binance-status situations (marked paid, completed, cancelled, appeal) cannot be changed, because replies are chosen by situation.
- KYC happens outside the chat, so a step worked out from our messages (the KYC steps; on buy orders details, paying, beneficiary activation) is a best guess: copilot-draft also offers the matched question's replies for the neighbouring steps (alternativeSteps, stepOptions; only replies that do not also apply at the current step) and names steps with no reply of their own for a one-tap switch. "Client can pay" is never offered before Binance's Verified notice or our payment details, and no reply that invites payment is offered as another step before verification. A reply sent unchanged for another step sets the chat to that step once Binance has the message (copilot_set_order_situation from the delivery callback, so the mark is newer than our message) and is saved under that step (copilot-learn situation, never out of KYC); the step to set is the one the chat will show after sending, when it is one of the reply's steps, because the approver knows the client's real step.
- "AI picks, Playbook speaks": copilot-draft shows a question's replies first without AI only when the best question for this side scores at least 0.60 and is 0.15 ahead of the next question, for this side or the other (two close questions, however high they score, are siblings the embeddings confuse), and of the lowest score returned when the lookup hit its limit; otherwise (a real client question, not small talk, with a candidate that has a reply that applies to this order now or in another situation) the utility model picks one of the top same-side questions scoring at least 0.40, or none, and only a high or medium pick is shown first; when the model cannot be asked or gives no valid answer (over the budget, failed, an answer outside the candidates, small talk) every question at match_threshold is shown first as before. mode 'context' returns the order facts only (the panel's request when nothing waits), never matching or AI, and has its own rate allowance. A chat state being drafted by another request is waited for until that request may be taken over (the 202 says when), never paid twice. The model never writes reply text; its call is logged as purpose 'judge', variant 'route' (no new purpose, so no database change), it is skipped when it would pass the monthly cap, and drafting again for the same chat state reuses the pick. Sending the picked reply adds the client's wording to that question, so the next similar message matches without AI.
- Binance's extra-KYC field (additionalKycVerify, from raw_data/order_detail_raw or the screen's live order detail, which the screen sends as it is; the highest value wins) fills a KYC notice missing from the chat (not synced yet, or missed; Binance's KYC notices are read on their own besides the last 80 messages): 1 means KYC is required from the order's creation; 2 means verified as of our payment details, else our latest KYC step (a KYC request or the verification-call message), never later, so a KYC request after our payment details still means a re-check and an approver's correction made after that step still wins (our small talk never undoes it). An approver's "Client can pay" set by hand, or move from "Client can pay" back to a KYC step (a re-check, told by copilot_situation_marks.detected_code), says the client was verified by then: the verification counts from our latest KYC step before the newest such correction, so our KYC messages after it are a re-check, as after Binance's notice or our payment details. While the field says 1 and the chat shows neither the Verified notice nor our payment details, a "Client can pay" set by hand does not hold (the panel's step menu blocks it). A sell order with no KYC sign in the chat is "Client can pay" only when the field is known to be 0; while it is not known it is KYC pending. The panel words the pay deadline by the server's step decision (before_verification); Binance's live field only adds caution (a live "required" the answer did not know), and a new "verified" or "no extra KYC" waits for the facts to be reloaded, which the panel does at once. The chat's own notices win when present, and filing an earlier message uses "required" only. Deadlines (notifyPayEndTime while TRADING, confirmPayEndTime once BUYER_PAYED; never one for the other) come from the screen's live detail first, then the stored detail, and are read as single fields because those payloads hold personal data. complainFreezeTime is not used: the release monitor reads it as the complaint window closing, CP-6 as it opening, and that is not settled. Before verification the drafts get the pay deadline only as "order expires" (never "time left to pay"); our appeal case is never told to the drafts. Client facts reach the AI only as counts and yes/no (phone on file, documents, earlier orders, appeals on the client's orders as the client card counts them, name check label).
- A question (copilot_playbook_topics) owns its phrasings; each reply covers situations through scope.stages, where a group code (sell.kyc) covers its steps, including steps added later, so approved replies are re-pointed, never thrown away, when situations change. A reply whose situation code no longer exists is never offered until one is picked again.
- Each approver send or approve saves the masked chat before the reply as an example (copilot_playbook_examples, latest 12 per reply) and adds the client's wording to the question; replies are ranked by how closely the current chat matches their examples, and the saving judge sees them to tell a variant (different situation) from a conflict (same situation).
- Editing one language copy marks the other out of date (entries trigger); an out-of-date copy is never offered (the other language is shown with a note) until it is rewritten from the other language or confirmed.
- Phrasings and example chats without an embedding are embedded in the same call as an approver's next chat lookup, after an approver's save, or when a question is opened on the Playbook page (copilot-learn index); there is no background embedding job.
- The old Copilot (copilot-suggest, copilot-teach, copilot-train, style exemplars, blacklist, hourly mining) is retired; its tables are kept as history and nothing reads them.
